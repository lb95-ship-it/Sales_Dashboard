Detailing scriptsstructure only

NEPQ-style scripts, per product and per audience

A script here would be built for one product and one kind of audience — an OD who already writes it, an OD who has never heard of it, an ophthalmologist weighing it against what they use now — and would follow the same stages each time:

  1. Connection Open without pitching. Earn the next two minutes.
  2. Situation What they reach for now, and for which patient.
  3. Problem awareness Where that current choice costs them something.
  4. Consequence What it costs the patient or the practice if nothing changes.
  5. Solution awareness The product, framed against the gap they just described.
  6. Commitment A specific next step — a patient type to try it on, not “think about it”.

Before any script ships

Anecdotes must be real. Several stages above have a slot for a story — another practice’s result, an outcome you heard about. Those slots stay empty until you fill them from an actual account. In front of a clinical audience an invented anecdote is not a small embellishment; it is a claim about patient outcomes, and it will not survive one follow-up question.

Objection handlingstructure only

A reference you can open in the parking lot

One entry per objection you actually hear — cost, coverage, “I already use something for that”, compounding-pharmacy hesitancy, turnaround time — each with the response that has worked and the one that has not. This is a lookup, not a script: the value is finding the right one in fifteen seconds between visits.

Nothing is seeded here on purpose. The list is worth building from objections you have actually logged; a generic set would look finished while being wrong for your territory.
